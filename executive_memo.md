# MetroPulse: Executive Memo

**To:** Leadership
**From:** MetroPulse Analytics
**Re:** Two recommended operational initiatives, based on Apr-Jun 2024 NYC trip data analysis

## Recommendation

We recommend funding two pilots this quarter:

1. **Off-Manhattan Demand Development (Brooklyn)**
2. **Airport-Focused Operations (JFK/LaGuardia)**

Both are backed by statistically validated evidence from a 9.2M-trip dataset
(Apr-Jun 2024), not assumption.

## Evidence

**Initiative 1 - Brooklyn:** Manhattan captures 88.76% of all pickups, while
Brooklyn - NYC's most populous borough - accounts for just 0.84% (854
trips/day). This is a large, quantified gap in an underserved, high-population
market.

**Initiative 2 - Airports:** Airport pickups average $76.45/trip vs $24.45 for
non-airport trips - a statistically significant $51.99 average difference
(95% CI: $51.93-$52.06). JFK alone is the #2 busiest single pickup zone
citywide (459K trips in 3 months).

## Expected Impact

- **Brooklyn:** Incremental trip volume in an underpenetrated market; reduces
  over-reliance on Manhattan (a concentration risk if Manhattan demand ever
  softens)
- **Airports:** Captures more of an already-proven, high-value trip category
  through better driver positioning at peak arrival windows

## Risks

- **Brooklyn:** Incentive costs may not be recovered if underlying demand is
  structurally lower (fewer viable fares even with better supply); guardrails
  on driver earnings and average fare will catch this early
- **Airports:** Pre-positioning drivers could increase idle time if arrival
  timing predictions are imprecise; guardrail on driver idle time will catch
  this early

## What We Explicitly Did NOT Recommend, and Why

- **Weather-triggered planning:** Statistically tested - rain/dry demand
  difference is not significant (95% CI includes 0). Contradicts the common
  assumption; recommending this initiative would contradict our own data.
- **Transit-disruption response:** No genuine subway disruption events were
  identifiable in the data (low-ridership days are regular weekly cyclicality,
  not disruptions), and taxi demand fell alongside subway on those days rather
  than compensating for it - no evidence of a substitution effect to exploit.

## Next Action

Launch both 4-week pilots in parallel (see full pilot design document for
treatment/control structure, metrics, and decision rules). Review results at
the 4-week mark against the pre-defined decision rules to determine whether to
scale, adjust, or discontinue each.
